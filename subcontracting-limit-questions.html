How much of a set-aside contract can you subcontract? Plain answers to the questions small contractors ask.

If you hold an 8(a), WOSB, SDVOSB, HUBZone or small business set-aside contract, you have agreed to do a share of the work yourself. These are the questions people ask about that limit, in their own words: what the 50 percent rule means, how to work out the percentage, what counts and what does not, when it is measured, what happens if you go over, and how to keep the records that prove your number.

The basics: what the subcontracting limit is and which contracts it covers

How much of a small business set-aside contract can I subcontract?

Under the limitations on subcontracting (FAR 52.219-14, implementing SBA's rule at 13 CFR 125.6), a small business prime on a services contract may pay no more than 50% of the amount the government pays it to subcontractors that are not similarly situated. People often call this the 50 percent rule or the self-performance requirement. Supplies are also 50%, general construction is 85% and special trade construction is 75%, with the cost of materials excluded for those three. The clause in your awarded contract is what governs.

Does the 50% rule apply to my contract?

SBA's rule covers full and partial small business set-asides above the simplified acquisition threshold, and it also lists 8(a), SDVOSB, VOSB, HUBZone and WOSB/EDWOSB contracts. The practical test is whether FAR 52.219-14, or an agency deviation of it, is incorporated in your awarded contract or order. The clause does not apply to the unrestricted portion of a partial set-aside. If you are unsure, ask your contracting officer before you build a subcontracting plan around an assumption.

Is the subcontracting limit different for 8(a), SDVOSB, WOSB and HUBZone contracts?

The percentages are the same across the programs; what changes is who counts as similarly situated. A similarly situated entity must hold the same program status that qualified you for the award (an 8(a) sub on an 8(a) contract, an SDVOSB sub on an SDVOSB set-aside, a WOSB sub on a WOSB set-aside, a HUBZone sub on a HUBZone award) and be small under the NAICS code you assigned to the subcontract. A sub that is merely small, but lacks the matching status, counts against your limit.

How does the limitation on subcontracting work for a joint venture?

Under 13 CFR 125.8 the joint venture as a whole must meet the applicable limitation on subcontracting, and the small business partner (or partners) must perform at least 40% of the work the joint venture performs, which must be more than administrative work. That 40% share is a separate test from the 50% limit. Read 13 CFR 125.8 and your joint venture agreement, and get advice for your specific arrangement.

Is the limitations on subcontracting the same as the ostensible subcontractor rule?

No. The ostensible subcontractor rule is part of SBA's size and affiliation regulations (13 CFR 121.103): it asks whether a subcontractor is really performing the primary and vital parts of the work, or whether the prime is unusually reliant on it, which can make the two firms affiliated for size purposes. The limitation on subcontracting is a payment-based percentage measured during performance. Staying under the percentage does not by itself answer an ostensible subcontractor question, so treat them as two separate issues.

How to calculate the percentage, and what counts

How do I calculate the limitations on subcontracting percentage?

Total the amounts the government has paid you under the contract for the measurement period. Total what you paid subcontractors that are not similarly situated for work on that contract, leaving out materials on supply and construction contracts. Divide the second number by the first and compare the result with the limit for your contract type. The free subcontracting limit calculator does this arithmetic in your browser, and the step-by-step calculation guide explains each input.

Do I use the amounts I invoiced or the amounts the government actually paid?

The rule is written in terms of the amount paid by the government, so the denominator is payments received for the period, not amounts invoiced, billed or accrued. Using invoices can make your percentage look lower or higher than it really is, especially near the end of a period when invoices are still unpaid. Keep the payment records that support each figure.

Why doesn't my subcontracting percentage match when I count hours or people?

Because the limit is measured in dollars paid, not labor hours, headcount or full-time equivalents. A sub with few hours but expensive specialized work can move the dollar percentage far more than its share of hours, and the reverse is also true. Hours belong to a different obligation, such as an agency's monthly workforce report, so keep the two calculations separate rather than deriving one from the other.

Do similarly situated subcontractors count toward the 50% limit?

Payments to a verified similarly situated entity do not count against your limit; FAR 52.219-14 treats that work as if you performed it. The status has to be verified and documented for each subcontractor, with the date and the evidence, rather than assumed from a name, a website or an old certification. If you cannot verify the status, the payments count. The similarly situated entity guide shows how to keep that evidence beside the numbers.

Does a similarly situated sub's own subcontracting (lower-tier or pass-through work) count?

Yes. Work that a similarly situated entity further subcontracts counts toward your limitation, so you need the lower-tier figure from that sub. For example, if a verified similarly situated sub receives $30,000 and passes $8,000 of that work to another firm, the $8,000 counts against you. Track the two tiers separately so the amount is neither missed nor counted twice.

Do 1099 independent contractors count as subcontractors under the limitation?

Yes. FAR 52.219-14 states that an independent contractor shall be considered a subcontractor, so payments to 1099 workers on the contract are subcontract dollars for this test. An independent contractor can be similarly situated only if it meets the same status and size requirements as any other first-tier subcontractor. Classify each person explicitly in your records rather than relying on how payroll happens to label them.

Are materials, travel or other direct costs excluded from the calculation?

On supply, general construction and special trade construction contracts, the cost of materials is excluded. On services contracts there is no general materials exclusion, but 13 CFR 125.6 excludes certain other direct costs that are not the principal purpose of the acquisition and that small businesses do not provide, such as airline travel, cloud computing services and mass media purchases. Those exclusions depend on facts about your contract, so confirm them with your reviewer or contracting officer; the free calculator does not model them.

Measurement period, deadlines, proof and penalties

Is the subcontracting limit measured monthly, yearly or over the whole contract?

Not monthly. For most contracts, compliance is measured by the end of the base term and then by the end of each option period, or, if the contracting officer chooses, by the end of each order's performance period. For orders issued under a multiple award contract, it is generally measured per order. Tracking the number every month is still sensible, because it shows where you are heading while there is time to adjust.

When is the deadline to be in compliance with the limitations on subcontracting?

FAR 52.219-14 sets the point of measurement as the end of the base term and of each option period, or the end of each order's performance period, as the contract specifies. There is no separate filing deadline in the clause, but a contracting officer can ask for the figures at any time. Check which option your contract uses and put the period end dates on your calendar.

How do I prove compliance with the limitations on subcontracting if my contracting officer asks?

Under 13 CFR 125.6, a contracting officer may require you to demonstrate compliance at any time during performance or at completion, using information such as invoices, copies of subcontracts or a list of the value of tasks performed. Be ready to show the calculation and the source of every input: government payment records, subcontractor payments allocated to this contract, dated similarly situated verification and lower-tier figures. SubLimit keeps that trail for you by tracing each contribution to its source row and listing every missing or unverified fact instead of guessing.

What is the penalty for exceeding the limitations on subcontracting?

13 CFR 125.6 provides that a violation is subject to the penalties in 15 U.S.C. 645(d), with a fine of the greater of $500,000 or the dollar amount paid to subcontractors in excess of the permitted level. The rule also treats compliance as a matter of contractor responsibility, so problems can affect future awards. If you think you are over or close to the limit, talk to your contracting officer and to counsel early.

Can going over the subcontracting limit hurt my CPARS past performance rating?

It can. FAR 42.1503 lists failure to comply with the limitations on subcontracting among the matters a past performance evaluation can address. Because evaluations follow you to future competitions, it is worth knowing your percentage well before the end of each period rather than reconstructing it afterward.

Tracking it: spreadsheets, software and the monthly workforce report

Is there a limitations on subcontracting spreadsheet template or Excel tracker?

Many small primes track the limit in Excel, and for one contract with a few verified subs that can be enough. A useful tracker has tabs for government payments, subcontract payments with their allocation and materials portion, status evidence with dates, and a summary that divides counted subcontracting by payments received. What is a monthly workforce report, and is it the same as the subcontracting limit?

Some contracts, such as those using the OCC's monthly workforce report attachment, require the contractor to report each month who worked on the contract and how many hours. That report is hours- and people-based for one month, while the subcontracting limit is payment-based over the period of performance; the same payroll records feed both, but neither proves the other. Yes.
